## The three litmus tests (SPEC Part I)

**1. Every number is traceable.** Neither page types a figure. Concept 9 names
`totalRuns`, `totalSessions`, `costedSessions`, `totalSoftBreaks`; concept 10
names `totalBugsFiled`, `totalLedgerEntries`, `bugsFiled`, `ledgerEntries`. All
eight are recomputed by the harvest and checked by the evidence gate. The prose
gate refuses a two-digit number in any sentence on either page and passes.

**2. The failures are on the page.** Concept 9 is largely built out of them:
the store that lived in a git-ignored directory, so every run's telemetry died
with its working tree; the soft-break column that is empty for every run in the
store, which is why this site counts them from events instead; the denominator
that has to be named because sessions-that-exist and sessions-that-recorded-spend
are different populations; and the pane that reports its own failure because an
empty queue and an unreachable one look identical. Concept 10 carries the
lessons file that was a diary and duplicated itself, and the bug row that
outlived the session and not the run — an empty ledger reading as a clean one.

**3. Useful without Conductor.** Delete `inConductor` from concept 9 and what
remains is the logging-versus-observability distinction, the three quantities
that get skipped, the denominator rule, why read-only is a property rather than
a courtesy, and why an empty panel and a broken connection must not look alike.
Delete it from concept 10 and what remains is the asymmetry that writing costs
once while reading is charged forever, the argument for rules over narrative,
the bounded/deduped/one-writer requirements, lifetimes and scoping, and why an
empty memory is the most dangerous state. Both open `tryIt` with something that
needs nothing installed: `git log --format=...` and `cat AGENTS.md CLAUDE.md`.
