## The editorial decision worth recording

The checkpoint asks for one report on one run. The report cites three, and that
is the difference between a case study and a measurement.

The corpus holds three runs against the same fleet on consecutive days: an
ordinary backlog, then the coordinated round, then a second ordinary round. Same
repositories, same component library, same gates, same agents, same absent
supervisor. Exactly one variable moved — the shape of the work — so the middle
run's excess is attributable to coordination rather than to size, tooling or
luck. A single run's numbers can say what it cost and can never say what about
it was expensive.

Two findings fall out of that comparison and neither is visible in the round
alone:

- **Cost per checkpoint rose and cost per token fell.** The coordinated round's
  tokens-per-checkpoint is more than three times the backlog's, while its
  blended cost per million tokens is *lower*. Coordination is a volume cost, not
  a rate cost — it reads more, it is not charged more — and the two have
  completely different remedies. The report says so, because a reader deciding
  whether to attempt this needs to know which one they are looking at.
- **The dearest stage takes a larger share.** Better than a third of the
  coordinated round sits in one stage, against roughly a quarter in the two
  ordinary rounds either side. That is the stage where the shared change first
  lands and where every site's drift is discovered one difference at a time.

## Anonymisation, as the checkpoint defines it

- No client name, no domain, no site name, no identifying detail.
- No private repository name. The fleet is described by shape ("several small
  marketing sites share one component library"), and the report generalises past
  websites entirely in its second paragraph — anywhere one library is consumed
  by several near-identical applications, the same asymmetry holds.
- No field-note prose. Nothing is quoted; the numbers are recomputed.
- The dearest stage is published as a share, sessions and checkpoints, and
  deliberately not by name, because a plan's stage names describe a client's
  work. The report says that out loud rather than leaving the gap unexplained.
- `npm run anonymity` passes over the built output, which is where the rule is
  actually enforced.

The report also declines to treat its own clean sheet as good news. All three
runs closed every checkpoint and every gate came back green; the report says
that is not evidence of anything, points at the verification article for why,
and reads the bugs-filed figure instead — the one number in the store that only
exists because somebody chose to write it.
